# The longlist

Candidates considered for collection II, with notes. Being here does not mean being chosen. Kept so that the next collection starts from a landscape, not from a blank page.

Status: **chosen** · **reserve** (strong, waiting for the right collection) · **declined** (with the reason, so nobody re-litigates it without new information)

## Chosen for collection II, *Worthy of Notice*

| Story | Mode | Tone | Scale | Form | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **It Is Worthy of Notice**: Henrietta Leavitt, 1908–1923 | documented history | quiet, luminous | one sentence → the size of the universe | illustrated book + a blink comparator | The greatest understatement in science. A woman paid by the hour wrote "it is worthy of notice" about the ruler for the cosmos. Leaves behind the idea of the *standard candle*, and of noticing as an act. |
| **His Imperial Majesty**: Joshua Norton, San Francisco 1859–1880 | documented + legend, sorted | comic, then tender | one man, one city | picture book with glosses | A city co-authoring a fiction for twenty years. Lets the reader sort legend from record. Its question about who gets to declare themselves something is left open. |
| **Bread from Air**: Fritz Haber | documented history | dark, unresolved | one reaction → half of humanity | illustrated book, two tones | The same chemistry, the same man, the same years. Refuses both hagiography and villainy. The heaviest piece; it needs room. |
| **Tuesday, with Notes** | fiction | wry, then moving | one ordinary day | annotated diary with an edition lens | The founding brief's own image of looking back from a century on, turned into a mechanism. Shows how significance is *constructed* by later readers and contested by even later ones. |
| **The Same Face**: Kuleshov, Mozzhukhin | documented + contested science | playful, then unsettling | a glance | silent film with reader choices | The reader performs the experiment on themselves. Then the story admits the experiment may never have happened as told. Ends by turning outward to every face the reader will see. |

Range check: three eras; four countries; comic, tragic, wry and quiet; admirable, compromised, deluded and fictional protagonists; a book, a diary, a film. All five share a sensibility. None shares a structure.

## Reserve

- **The future is behind you.** Among Aymara speakers, gesture and language place the past ahead (seen) and the future behind (unseen) (Núñez & Sweetser 2006). This could be a short, strange, beautiful book, and possibly a companion to *Tuesday*. To guard against exoticism, it needs careful framing and consultation with sources by Aymara scholars.
- **The Mpemba effect.** Erasto Mpemba, a Tanzanian schoolboy (1963), was laughed at for saying hot ice-cream mix froze faster. The physics is still disputed decades on. This would be a story about being right, being wrong, and the effect possibly not being real. It needs care: the replication status is genuinely messy.
- **Frances Oldham Kelsey and thalidomide (1960).** She was a new FDA reviewer who kept asking for more data. Strong, but the "lone heroine" framing needs complicating. Hold until it can be told through the paperwork.
- **Wilson Bentley, "Snowflake" Bentley.** A Vermont farmer who photographed over 5,000 snowflakes. A gorgeous visual book, but tonally close to Leavitt. It belongs in a different collection.
- **The Antikythera mechanism.** A bronze computer lost for two millennia. The reconstruction is famously contested, which is good. It could be an interactive gear room.
- **Grace Bedell's letter to Lincoln (1860).** An eleven-year-old suggested the beard. Charming and documented. It is risky as a "small things matter" fable and needs an unexpected angle.
- **Stanislav Petrov, 26 September 1983.** The false-alarm night. Powerful but much retold, and the details are contested. Only a radically different form would justify it.
- **The Ben Franklin effect.** Franklin won over a rival by borrowing a book. The anecdote is real (Autobiography), and the psychology is debated. Could be a short two-page piece.
- **The Voynich manuscript.** Pure ambiguity. Would need an interaction where the reader tries to read it.
- **Johnny Appleseed's actual business** (land claims, cider). Legend versus record, like Norton. Hold to avoid two of the same mode in one collection.
- **The Great Stink of 1858 and Bazalgette.** A city forced into the future by smell. Comic and documented, and it suits a sewer cross-section illustration.
- **Rongorongo.** An undeciphered script from Rapa Nui. Requires deep care; possibly never right for us.
- **The Phantom Time hypothesis.** A crank theory as a lens on how we know dates. Fun, but the story is mostly the debunking.

## Declined

- **Edison's thousand failed light bulbs, Einstein failing maths, Michael Jordan cut from the team.** The motivational canon, and some of it false.
- **Phineas Gage.** Overtold, and the popular version overstates what is known.
- **The marshmallow test.** Replication and confounding problems, and it is frequently used to imply character judgements about readers.
- **Kitty Genovese and the "38 witnesses".** The popular version is substantially false. It could only be told as a story about how the false version spread, which is a different story and very dark.
- **Stanford prison experiment.** Seriously compromised as science, and it moralises too easily.

## Leads noticed during research (unverified, do not use without checking)

- Haber is often credited with the line "in peace time a scientist belongs to the world, but in war time he belongs to his country". It is widely repeated but no primary source was found.
- "Half the nitrogen atoms in your body came from the Haber–Bosch process." This is often stated, but the source traces back to a rough estimate. We use the population figure (Erisman et al. 2008) instead.
- "Hubble said Leavitt deserved the Nobel." Widely repeated; not found in a primary source.
